# ImageNet classification

## perc-imagenet-gpt4o

**Quantity.** One 224×224 center-cropped ImageNet image assigned one of 1,000 named classes, normalized from batches of 100. The original [vision evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01955v1), Table 1, reports GPT-4o top-1 accuracy 77.20%. Appendix F states batch size 100; Appendix I identifies gpt-4o-2024-08-06. The [implementation](https://github.com/EPFL-VILAB/fm-vision-evals/blob/main/taskit/tasks/classify.py) sends the class list once, a separate text instruction per image, a JSON schema and one image per instruction. [Image preprocessing](https://github.com/EPFL-VILAB/fm-vision-evals/blob/main/taskit/utils/data.py), crop_img, defaults to resizing the short side to224 and center cropping224. [Wrapper](https://github.com/EPFL-VILAB/fm-vision-evals/blob/main/taskit/mfm.py) uses high image detail and can retry up to three times.

**Human time: 26 seconds, a transferred median.** [Shankar et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/shankar20c/shankar20c.pdf), sections 2 and 4.3, reports five trained authors independently labeling a shuffled set of 1,000 original ImageNet and 1,000 ImageNetV2 images. The per-image median is 26 seconds. During evaluation, annotators used class search, 100 training exemplars per class and a labeling guide. Prior training is excluded. Three had reviewed evaluation images before a six-month gap; two had not.

The [supplement](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/shankar20c/shankar20c-supp.pdf), section D, identifies the recorded time as active labeling time. Figure 9 plots per-image medians, but the paper does not specify whether the overall 26 seconds pools individual timings or aggregates image medians. Retain its reported `median`; do not invent a more specific aggregation. Transfer it without adjustment to the AI's crop/setup: `transferred_timings`, `estimated`. The timing donor comprises 10,000 image-labeling attempts: five participants each labeled the 2,000-image evaluation set. Use `human_time_subset=all` and `human_attempts=10000`. The flagged-image exclusions concern the performance assessment; they are not a reported correct-label-only timing filter.

**Range: 26 to 64.8 seconds, with the central as the floor.** Shankar et al.
publish two readings of the same labeling effort in the same sentence: a per-image
median of 26 seconds and a **median labeling time of 36 hours for the entire
labeling task**. The task is 2,000 images, so the second is 36 x 3,600 / 2,000 =
**64.8 seconds an image** for the median labeler, the mean pace behind the same
work. The two differ because the distribution is heavily right-skewed: the
supplement's Figure 9 omits 24 images whose median time exceeded 400 seconds, and
the paper says labelers spent "anywhere from a couple of seconds to 40 minutes"
on one image. The median is therefore the floor of the two published readings and
the implied mean is the ceiling. Added 2026-09-17.

**Performance: below the trained cohort.** Supplement Table 4, All Images / Original, gives human top-1 percentages 76.9, 79.1, 80.7, 83.5 and 90.3. Their equal-weight mean is **82.1%**, versus **77.2%** for GPT-4o. The 4.9-point gap and four higher-scoring humans support `below` for this baseline; this is not a universal threshold. Overlap with the lowest individual does not establish cohort parity. Use the original-ImageNet column, not the V2 or multi-label scores.

The [original scoring code](https://github.com/modestyachts/evaluating_machine_accuracy_on_imagenet/blob/master/human_accuracy.py), `compute_acc_dict` and `grade_predictions(top1=True)`, compares one predicted label to the official label and excludes flagged problematic images. Thus `different_assessment` covers the different evaluation subsets; `different_inputs_or_tools` covers crops and human reference materials. No top-five conversion remains. The author's [notebook](https://github.com/modestyachts/evaluating_machine_accuracy_on_imagenet/blob/master/HumanAccuracyNotebook.ipynb) links raw annotations, but that public S3 object returned HTTP 403 on 2026-09-12. The published table and timing suffice; no raw count or mean is reconstructed.

**Compute estimate, not billing-token FLOPs.** No architecture is disclosed. Use a transparent visual transformer proxy: a14-pixel patch grid produces (224/14)^2=256 visual positions, a2B-parameter visual encoder and256 projected positions processed by the50B-active language model. The identifiable reference is [OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14](https://huggingface.co/laion/CLIP-ViT-bigG-14-laion2B-39B-b160k), whose [published vision configuration](https://github.com/mlfoundations/open_clip/blob/main/src/open_clip/model_configs/ViT-bigG-14.json) has 224-pixel images, 14-pixel patches, 48 layers, width 1,664 and MLP ratio 4.9231. Its main vision-block weights are approximately 48×(4+2×4.9231)×1664²=1.84B parameters, with small embedding/projection/norm terms. Rounding to 2B is a proportionate vision-backbone proxy; the reference model's text tower is not included. Projecting each patch to a language-model position remains an additional assumption, not a fact about OpenCLIP or GPT-4o. This is an architectural assumption; it is not an assertion that the API's255 billed image tokens are256 internal positions. At this small resolution encoder attention is a minor term. A different patch grid or visual resampling scheme would change the estimate.

The actual published class list has13,480 characters for1,000 labels. At an assumed4 characters/text token, this contributes33.7 tokens/image in a100-image batch. Allocate approximately45 tokens/image for the repeated instruction,20 for the per-image schema entry,10 for JSON label output, and11.3 for system wording/message framing: **120 text positions/image**. This estimate deliberately includes schema and repeated instructions. It spans the several published prompt variants, whose exact selected number is not identified by the paper. Total language-model positions=256+120=376. Add a2% allowance for retried model calls (implementation permits retries, frequency not reported). CSV text tokens=120×1.02=122.4 input+output tokens. Projected visual workload=256×1.02=261.12 positions, so the language model processes 383.52 total positions. The separate encoder also processes 261.12 visual positions. Neither visual workload is added to CSV text tokens.

Compute=1.02×[2×50e9×376 + 2×2e9×256] = **3.939648e13 FLOPs/image**. Method operation_count; tokens=122.4 contains text only. The visual-position workloads remain in this calculation, so removing them from the text-token field does not reduce FLOPs. No separate helper model is required by classification. A factor-of-two sensitivity is appropriate for hidden architecture and retry assumptions. This is normalized batch compute, not the cost of making a separate one-image call with the full class list repeated.
