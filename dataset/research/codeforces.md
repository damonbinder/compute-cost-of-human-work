# A defined hard Codeforces problem

## reas-cf-o3

**Selection and performance.** Use one concrete 2900-rated problem from the source evaluation: [1965D, Missing Subarray Sum](https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/1965/D?locale=en). Given all but one of the subarray sums of a positive palindromic array of length at most 1,000, produce any consistent palindrome. Completion means accepted code passing the problem's full test suite. This is an illustrative hard problem, not an estimate for all tasks near o3's rating and not a random representative sample.

[Competitive Programming with Large Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06807v1), Appendix B/Table 1, reports 1965D with 690/1,162 unranked samples passing and a solved ranked outcome with zero failed submissions. The configuration behind its 2724 rating ranks 1,162 candidates and allows up to 10 submissions; Appendix B also states that submission timing is assigned from human medians for rating purposes. Those synthetic timing assignments are not AI runtime or active human solution time. The early Codeforces checkpoint differs from the later IOI checkpoint. All 1,162 candidates, including failures and unused successes, enter the compute estimate; no 10-sample shortcut and no use of the later 1,000-sample IOI configuration.

**Human estimate=120 minutes=7,200 seconds.** The baseline is an experienced competitive programmer, approximately 2400–2800 rating, working without an editorial or AI. This is an estimate of reaching the same accepted-code endpoint as the reported successful AI run, so performance=match by construction, not a measured human success rate. It is not the average of all humans including indefinite failures.

The [original contest](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/128833) gave two hours for six problems. That time limit is only a scale check; I do not divide it by six or assign all two hours automatically to D. Inspecting the [setter's solution code](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/128914), the difficult insight is to use parity of multiplicities of reflected subarray sums to recover the central sums, distinguish missing central versus noncentral sums, and reconstruct the palindrome. Implementation additionally needs sorting/multiset cancellation and verification of derived candidates. A reasoned allocation is 10 min interpreting the reconstruction conditions and examples; 65 min finding/proving the parity and missing-sum case structure; 25 min implementing it; 20 min tests/debugging including repeated sums, odd/even length and large integers. Total 120 min. The bounds move the two components that actually vary. If the parity observation comes quickly the allocation is 10, 25, 25 and 20 minutes, 75 minutes in all, which is also about what a contestant who solved D inside the two-hour six-problem window can have spent on it. If the insight takes twice as long and the implementation needs a debugging round it is 10, 130, 30 and 40 minutes, 210 minutes. Many experienced people need longer than that or fail. No source records this component timing. The source's contest setting makes hour-scale problem solving plausible, while the inspected algorithm makes a few minutes implausible for the stated experienced rather than world-leading baseline.

**Compute estimate.** Parameters are assumed 50B active (fresh-five-models.md), following the corrected central GPT-4o-scale transfer. There is no separate source basis to retain 100B for this early o3 checkpoint. The same size transfer applies to its unidentified scorer; both coefficients change together. Per candidate estimate 1,000 prompt tokens, 7,000 reasoning tokens and 1,000 implementation/test output tokens: 9,000 positions. The roughly 100-line setter code gives a useful scale for final implementation, but the 7,000 reasoning allocation is judgment for several nontrivial invariant/case-analysis stages and trial reasoning, not a measured chain. Model-generated tests and refinements are included in that allocation. Use a broad 4k–16k output sensitivity. The scorer is undisclosed: assume one full forward scoring pass over prompt+candidate and one scoring token, with the primary coefficient; this is conservative relative to sharing hidden states or ranking only final code, but necessary rather than omitting ranking.

Generation=1,162×9,000=10,458,000 positions. Ranking=1,162×9,001=10,459,162. Total=20,917,162 positions, ×1e11 = **2.0917162e18 FLOPs**. This analytic estimate includes all selected system work for one problem; ai_attempts=not_applicable (the 1,162 samples are internal search, not 1,162 full system runs). Compute_subset=not_applicable avoids pretending the workload was measured. Model size and candidate/scorer length dominate uncertainty. Ranking-only-final-code at 2,001 positions/candidate would reduce total to 1.2783162e18; at fixed scorer rule, halving/doubling generated length materially changes the result. Public test execution is ordinary software and excluded from AI-model FLOPs.

AI full-test submissions versus human pretests is different_inputs_or_tools. The estimated human includes failed ideas/debugging; the AI includes discarded candidate work. The result must not be extrapolated as representative compute or human time for a 2900-rated problem distribution.
