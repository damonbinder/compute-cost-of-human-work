# Warehouse item picking: an explicit architecture proxy

## robo-binpick

The unit is one successful transfer of an ordinary, graspable retail item from a mixed tote to its destination tray or tote, including machine vision and a post-pick check. It excludes fetching the container or resolving a difficult exception. [Amazon's Sparrow introduction](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/operations/amazon-introduces-sparrow-a-state-of-the-art-robot-that-handles-millions-of-diverse-products) establishes this deployed capability, but does not disclose its model graph. This candidate estimates a Sparrow-class system using related Amazon research components; it does not label research benchmark models as the deployed Sparrow implementation.

### Evidence and proxy choice

The original [ARMBench paper](https://cdn.amazon.science/16/f3/35fa6104443b86fd52d82640ec94/armbench-an-object-centric-benchmark-dataset-for-robotic-manipulation.pdf), saved as `agent-work/sources/robotics-science/armbench-2023.pdf`, documents top-down pick imagery, multiple transfer views, and a place image. Its tasks cover instance segmentation, object identity and defect detection. Sections IV–VI provide Mask R-CNN with a ResNet-50 backbone, ResNet-50 retrieval and ResNet-50 image classification baselines. These are reproducible computational analogues of the needed functions, not demonstrated parity models. The paper also notes human monitoring and exception resolution; the selected ordinary successful transfer does not include those exceptions.

The original [2025 Amazon multi-suction paper](https://cdn.amazon.science/af/04/c6c8435a44349bae91eced804dfc/demonstrating-multi-suction-item-picking-at-scale-via-multi-modal-learning-of-pick-success.pdf), saved as `agent-work/sources/robotics-science/amazon-multisuction-2025.pdf`, includes both offline prediction evaluation and a real-world deployment of approximately 17,000 pick attempts over one week (Section V.C.2). It reports relative reductions of multipick, mispick and amnesty, not absolute successful-transfer rates.

The demonstrated configuration uses local crops, RGB/depth and a pick-location image. The central proxy here deliberately selects the paper's alternative global RGB/depth configuration with shared visual encoding and cross-attention (Table V and global-image discussion). A full-tote visual embedding reused for candidate scoring is an appropriate computational analogue for selecting an accessible item from a mixed tote. This does not claim the deployed trial used the alternative configuration. The heavier segmentation/identity pipeline dominates the total, so the choice has a limited numerical effect unless many local visual encoders are run.

Assume ten accessible visible items, with four plausible suction candidates per item (two contact locations and two orientations/cup choices), or **40 candidate scores**. This is a task-specific workload assumption, not a measured scene count. It reflects a small set of accessible surfaces rather than an exhaustive pose search. The paper's batch of 128 measures inference latency on a test batch, so it is not used to establish scene workload. Varying the candidate count from 10 to 200 affects only the lightweight per-candidate head in this shared-encoder proxy.

### FLOP calculation

`count_sparrow_proxy.py` and `research/sparrow-proxy-operations.json` contain the complete analytic calculation. The [Matterport implementation](https://github.com/matterport/Mask_RCNN), explicitly identified in ARMBench, supplies the FPN/RPN and head structure. Its default inference settings use 1024-square padded images, 1000 proposals and 100 final mask slots. We override the backbone to the paper's ResNet-50 and use three classes for object, container and background segmentation. ResNet downsampling follows this implementation's first-convolution stride. Count multiply and add separately.

One segmentation view costs 618.210 GFLOPs: backbone 161.162, lateral FPN 16.106, spatial FPN 102.677, RPN 207.566, box head 27.818 and mask head 102.881. The RPN has 512 hidden channels; using 256 would materially undercount it. Three segmentation views are assumed: one pick and two transfer views. ARMBench Section V uses a pick query and up to three transfer queries, with segmented objects, supporting these processing stages. Two transfer views are a central coverage choice for identity and defect checks; their exact deployment count is undisclosed. The recorded place image is not charged a full segmentation pass because the cited methods do not establish one.

The MultiMAE-base proxy uses 12 layers, width 768, 196 patches per modality plus one global position. Its transformer work is 12×(24×393×768² + 4×393²×768), plus input projection: 72.761 GFLOPs. The architecture is supported by the [authors' base-model implementation](https://github.com/EPFL-VILAB/MultiMAE/blob/main/multimae/multimae.py); this is not a Sparrow parameter disclosure. Cached key/value projections and 40 cross-attention pick queries add 1.036 GFLOPs. Add five 224-square ResNet-50 crop passes at 7.712 GFLOPs each: three identity-query embeddings (one pick-image object crop and one object crop from each of the two transfer views), plus two defect-classification passes (one crop from each transfer view). Identity and defect classifiers have distinct weights, so those transfer crops incur separate passes. Gallery identity embeddings are precomputed; no per-pick gallery image re-encoding is assumed.

Subtotal = 1,966,984,601,600 FLOPs. Add 10% for normalization, image warps, geometric grasp/motion evaluation and other helpers, then 10% expected extra processing for occasional reacquisition or unsuccessful grasp attempts. Total = **2,380,051,367,936 FLOPs**. Both allowances are assumptions. The estimate does not turn integer search into FLOPs.

Segmentation dominates. One pick plus one to three transfer views and a factor-of-two change in per-view architecture imply roughly 0.8–6 TFLOPs per item, rather than narrow precision. Omitting all identity/defect/grasp helpers changes the central estimate little; replacing the heavy segmentation proxy with a lightweight production model could change it substantially. This is the principal evidence limitation for independent review. We have not inferred compute from price, cycle time, or a commercial throughput claim.

### Human baseline and quality

Estimate **5 seconds** for an ordinary person to make the same successful, undamaged transfer within arm's reach: visually choose an accessible item and reach (about 1.5 s), secure and move it (2 s), release and check it (1.5 s). This is an active handling estimate, not a sustained shift productivity target or an inventory-search task. **Range 3.5 to 8 seconds**, added 2026-09-17, re-running the same three steps at their ends: choose and reach 1-2.5 s depending on whether an accessible item is on top or has to be looked for, secure and move 1.5-3 s on the item's size and grip, release and check 1-2.5 s on whether the check is a glance or a turn of the item. It matches the selected successful transfer by construction, without claiming equal autonomous exception rates. Gripper/sensor and human-hand inputs differ. Count neither estimated human nor AI duration as a recorded sample. The 10% AI extra-work allowance retains modest retries on the path to one completed item.

No exact deployed model release or parameter count is established; leave release fields blank and mark shared token/parameter coefficients not applicable. The model ID explicitly identifies the architecture-proxy basis.

The source trial's relative defect reductions do not establish an absolute retry rate. The 10% extra-attempt allowance remains a modest explicit assumption and does not cover difficult human-resolved exceptions. A post-pick check can miss defects.
