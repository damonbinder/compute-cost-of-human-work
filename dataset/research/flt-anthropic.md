## Summary

One candidate row: `reas-flt-lean-anthropic-internal`. An unnamed Anthropic internal research model,
described only as "roughly comparable to Claude Fable 5.1", ran on a Claude Code harness over the
Prove2Me platform for 11 days from 7 August 2026 and produced the first complete machine-checked Lean 4
proof of Fermat's Last Theorem: 29,511 theorems, no `sorry`, resting on Lean's three standard axioms,
checked by the Lean kernel, by leanprover/comparator against a Mathlib-only statement of the theorem,
and by nanoda, a second Rust kernel, on a build Anthropic patched. Anthropic published one compute
number and one only: about six billion output tokens.

Recorded values: **compute_flops 6.25e21**, **tokens 3.13e10** (`input_cache_creation_output`),
**human_time 9.99288e7 seconds** (27,758 active expert hours, 13.9 person-years at the
2,000-hour convention), `performance_vs_human = match` by construction. That works out to
1.9e14 FLOPs per human-second.

Two estimates carry the row, and both are stated as ranges.

- **The input side of the compute.** The published figure is output-only, so it is a floor of 1.2e21
  FLOPs and nothing more. The central applies k = 1 + r with r = 4.21 appended tokens per output
  token, a per-turn composition ratio transferred from Meta FAIR's textbook formalization. Two bands:
  the donor's own append-profile band, 3.5 to 5.3, and a transfer band on whether the FLT harness's
  tool-result-to-output composition resembles Meta's at all, k = 3 to 10, giving 3.6e21 to 1.2e22. The
  appended basis is selected over the gross one only because the Claude Code harness cached, which is
  inferred from the harness description rather than observed, and is worth a factor of 28.6: the gross
  figure is 1.8e23.
- **The human side.** No timing sample exists, so the value estimates the active time a qualified
  human team would need for the same verified deliverable: 27,758 active hours central, 13.9
  person-years at 2,000 hours, with a range of 11,103 to 92,527 hours, built from the Imperial
  College FLT project's **observed commit record** — 727 distinct non-bot author-days — rather
  than from its funding. The two earlier routes both priced funded staffing and were linear in
  the same assumed FTE, which is why they agreed.

A reviewer should go at the caching premise first, the r = 4.21 transfer second and the 0.55
fraction-of-the-reduction-delivered third. Everything else in the row is either reported by Anthropic or measured directly from the

### The work unit

`Theorems/Thm_fermat_last_theorem.lean` declares

    theorem fermat_last_theorem (n : ℕ) (hn : 3 ≤ n) (a b c : ℕ) (ha : 0 < a) (hb : 0 < b) (hc : 0 < c) :
      a ^ n + b ^ n ≠ c ^ n

and the default build target `FinalCheck.lean` carries a `#guard_msgs` assertion that
`#print axioms fermat_last_theorem` returns exactly `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`, so the
build fails unless the proof rests on those three axioms alone, with no `sorry`, no added `axiom` and
no `native_decide`. `FinalCheck.lean` also derives Mathlib's own `FermatLastTheorem` from it. I fetched
both files and confirmed the text.

The proof follows Darmon, Diamond and Taylor's 1995 exposition of the Wiles and Taylor–Wiles argument:
Mazur's torsion theorem in the case needed for the irreducibility of the Frey curve's mod p
representation, Langlands–Tunnell at the prime 3, Wiles's 3–5 switch, the modularity lifting step
(R = T), Ribet's level lowering down to level 2, the Eichler–Shimura congruence, and Fontaine theory for
flat deformations. Buzzard's independent reading is that the argument as formalized covers p ≥ 17, and
that the remaining exponents are already carried by flt-regular's theorem for regular primes, the
smallest irregular prime being 37. The published `fermat_last_theorem` is unconditional over ℕ, so both
pieces are inside the artifact.

Dependencies assumed rather than proved: Mathlib v4.33.0 on Lean 4.33.1, pinned by commit in
`lakefile.lean`. Nothing else. The kernel checks everything beneath the statement, so the trusted base
is the Lean kernel (or nanoda) plus the checking tools, and comparator establishes that every constant
the statement mentions is identical to stock Mathlib's.

Reuse matters for reading the run, though not for the compute arithmetic. `ATTRIBUTION.md` lists 106
files containing Lean text taken or adapted from the Imperial College London FLT project or flt-regular
(90 of them from ICL FLT; 54 under `Definitions/`, 52 under `P2M/Sol/`), and 23 files that reproduce
Mathlib text. Anthropic also reports that a number of earlier attempts failed before the switch to
Prove2Me, and that those failed efforts contributed about 7% of the non-boilerplate lines in the final
proof.

Human input inside the run was the one-line statement of the goal theorem plus occasional priority
nudges from Tianyi Peng, quoted in full in the report as "Jacobian as a scheme sounds high priority" and
"push [the] Mazur [theorem] to be done soon". The report states that humans "wrote no mathematics and no
Lean beyond the one-line statement of the goal theorem".

### Model identity

This is the weakest structural part of the row, and it cannot be fixed with more research.

The model is an internal Anthropic research model that was never released, never named, and
characterized only by comparability to Claude Fable 5.1. Fable 5.1 itself was announced in September
2026, with the accompanying system card dated 1 September 2026, which is after the run ended on 17
August. Neither Fable 5 nor Fable 5.1 appears in the project's `models.csv`; a new record is minted in
`candidates/flt-anthropic/models.csv` for the internal model, with Fable 5.1 recorded as the stated
comparability anchor rather than as the model.

The coefficient is the dataset's shared frontier closed-model prior: 100B active parameters,
2e11 FLOPs per token, `active_parameters_basis = estimated`. That is the same value carried by
`claude-opus-4-5`, `claude-opus-4-8`, `claude-opus-5` and `gpt-6-astra`, so the row stays comparable
with the existing 1,410. The scenario range is 30B to 300B active, which moves the recorded value from
4.3e21 to 4.3e22.

One tokenizer point that a reviewer should hold on to. Anthropic's pricing documentation states that
Claude 4.7 and later models use a newer tokenizer producing "approximately 30% more tokens for the same
text". Fable 5.1 sits in that era, and the Meta donor run used Opus 4.5, which does not. The transferred
ratio is a ratio of token counts and is roughly tokenizer-neutral, but the FLOPs coefficient is applied
per token, so the same Lean text costs about 30% more recorded FLOPs under this run's tokenizer than it
would under the donor's. The effect is inside the recorded value, not an adjustment to it.

## reas-flt-lean-anthropic-internal

### Compute

The published record gives output tokens only. `compute_flops` must represent total processed tokens,
so the input side has to be estimated, and the output-only figure is kept as an explicit floor.

**Floor.** 6e9 output tokens × 2e11 FLOPs per token = **1.2e21 FLOPs**. This counts no prompt, no system
or tool definition, no Lean compiler output fed back to an agent, no Prove2Me card text, and no cache
creation. It is a bound, not an estimate.

**The donor.** Meta FAIR's "Automatic Textbook Formalization" (arXiv:2604.03071) is the only published
multi-agent Lean formalization run that reports both token counts and an explicit model of how much of
its input total is cache re-reads. It reports "a total of 83B input tokens (with multiple counting in
multi-turn dialogs) and 561M output tokens", $430K without caching, and a back-of-the-envelope "$100K
overall, $14K out of which for output tokens in both cases".

The parenthesis is the whole story, and Appendix A spells it out. With N agents, T turns each and m
tokens added per turn, the gross count is C = N·m·T(T+1)/2, because every turn re-reads the whole
prefix. The distinct positions the model actually forwards are only

    N·L = N·T·m = 2C / (T + 1)

and that is exactly what the params_tokens rule counts: every position once, as fresh input or cache
creation, cache reads excluded. N·L already contains the run's output positions, since an assistant
message joins the prefix of the next turn. N cancels, so the ratio turns on T alone, and the paper
states T ≈ 54.8.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Gross input C | 8.30e10 |
| Appended share 2/(T+1) | 0.0358 |
| Counted tokens N·L | 2.975e9 |
| Counted tokens per output token | 5.30 |
| Counted input per output token | 4.30 |
| Tokens added per turn, m | 1810 |
| Output tokens per turn | 341 |
| Input tokens per turn | 1468 |

Evaluating Appendix A's own price formulas at Claude Opus 4.5 list prices confirms the structure rather
than assuming it: P_nocache + output = $415,000 + $14,025 = $429,025 against the published $430K, 0.23%
out, and P_cache + output = 0.1 × $415,000 + 3 × $14,875 + $14,025 = $100,149 against the published
$100K, 0.15% out. Sensitivity to T: 30 turns would give 9.55, 40 would give 7.22, 70 would give 4.17,
90 would give 3.25.

A first draft of this note reached 8.4 to 13.8 instead, by inverting the cost ratio for a fresh share
after substituting the 5-minute cache's 1.25× write price into a dollar figure the paper computed at the
one-hour cache's cost. Appendix A sets c_store = 2·c_in, so appended tokens cost three times the base
rate, not 1.25 or 2. Redone at 3×, the same inversion returns 5.29 when c_hit is charged across the
whole of C and 5.47 when it is charged on C − N·L, both within 3% of the geometry route's 5.30. The two
routes agree; the 8.4 to 13.8 figure is discarded, and is recorded in
`agent-work/sources/flt-anthropic/comparable-run-meta-textbook.md` and in `calculations.json` only so it is
identifiable as an error if it surfaces in an earlier draft.

**What is being transferred.** A per-turn composition ratio, and nothing about dialog length. Writing
the donor's geometry out, r = N·L / output = (N·T·m)/(N·T·o) = m/o, so **T cancels**, and

    k = 1 + r = 2 + (tool-result tokens per turn) / (output tokens per turn)

T enters only on the donor side, where C and the output total are reported and T must be supplied to
recover m. An FLT agent whose dialogs run twice as long as Meta's, at the same tool-result and output
volume per turn, has exactly the same r and the same k; what changes is its gross count, which the
appended basis excludes. Revisions 1 and 2 of this row expressed the central, the band and both
direction arguments in target dialog length, which was dimensionally wrong; the independent review
caught it and the framing is gone.

The transfer assumption, stated properly: **the FLT harness appends about 3.2 tokens of tool result,
card text and system prompt per token the model emits**, which is what Meta's agents did.

**The caching premise, which is the largest lever in the row.** The appended basis is selected over the
gross one because the harness cached. That is worth a factor of 28.6, and it is inferred rather than
observed: Anthropic published no cache counters. The evidence is that Anthropic describes the harness as
"a Claude Code-based multi-agent harness", and Claude Code runs against the Anthropic API, where
automatic caching "moves the cache point forward automatically as conversations grow. Each new request
caches everything up to the last cacheable block, and previous content is read from cache." Meta's
RepoProver went through the OpenAI-compatibility layer, which supports no prompt caching, which is why
`DECISIONS.md` records that row on the gross basis. Earlier revisions dismissed the gross figure on the
ground that the params_tokens rule excludes it. That was wrong as stated: the rule excludes cache
*reads*, and where no cache exists a re-read position is a real forward pass. Applied to the donor it
would have overturned the donor's own central value. The gross figure is now carried as a scenario
conditioned on this premise failing.

**Two bands, not one.** The donor's 3.5 to 5.3 is an **append-profile** band at the donor's fixed T,
from a front-loaded opening block to linearly growing appends. It is a band on the donor's own r. The
larger uncertainty, whether the FLT harness's tool-result-to-output composition resembles Meta's at all,
is separate: the two differ in scaffold, model generation, agent count by three orders of magnitude, and
in what a turn consists of. A tool-result-to-output ratio from about 1 to about 8 gives **k = 3 to 10**.

**Which way it points.** One consideration down, two up, and one that earlier revisions argued in both
directions and which is void.

- **Down.** Meta's agents were short-output and file-read-heavy, 341 output tokens against 1,468
  tool-result tokens a turn. A proving run emitting whole Lean proof bodies and extended thinking per
  turn divides a similar tool-result volume into more output. At twice Meta's output per turn, r = 3.15.
- **Up.** Session restarts and compaction rebuild the prefix as new appended positions, re-paying the
  system prompt, tool definitions and re-read files each time. Anthropic's own account of the earlier
  failed campaign, that agents "quickly lost track of the project's state", is what restarts look like
  from outside.
- **Up.** Prove2Me maintains a DAG of theorem statements with natural-language descriptions that agents
  read to choose what to attempt, plus search over them. That is per-turn tool-result volume Meta's
  agents did not carry on top of their own file reads.
- **Void.** Dialog length, on the argument above.

**Structural evidence from the published record.** Earlier revisions said no turn, session or agent
count was published. That was wrong: the blog says "Dozens of Claude agents collaborated". Combined with
the other two published numbers, 6e9 output tokens over 11 days is 6,313 output tokens per second in
aggregate, which at 24, 48 and 96 agents is 263, 132 and 66 sustained output tokens per second per
agent. The low end of "dozens" is not achievable by one sequential stream, so either concurrency ran
well above the named agent count or each named agent drove many parallel sessions. It does not fix r,
but it does say the run was output-dense rather than idling on long reads.

`tokens_accounting` is `input_cache_creation_output`: fresh input plus cache creation plus output, cache

**Accounting limitations, all one-sided upward.**

- The 6e9 output figure covers the successful Prove2Me run. The earlier failed attempts contributed
  about 7% of the final non-boilerplate lines but are not described as inside that count, so their
  compute is unaccounted.
- Anthropic does not say whether the 6e9 includes extended thinking tokens. Anthropic's API counts
  thinking inside output tokens, so on the natural reading it does; if it does not, the figure is low.
- Lean compilation, the comparator replay and the nanoda check are conventional computation, not neural
  work, and are excluded. They are large in wall-clock terms and nil in FLOPs under this schema.
- No helper model is identified. If a smaller model handled triage or search, the total is overstated
  by whatever share it carried; the row uses the primary model's coefficient throughout, which is the
  explicit assumption COLUMNS permits.
