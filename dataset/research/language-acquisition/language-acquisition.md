## Work unit and human learning target

Each point estimates one model's complete training from random initialization to its reported BLiMP capability. The human comparison is an ordinary literate learner, with an established first language but no usable English grammar, learning English lexical support and grammar to approximately the model's **paired grammatical discrimination** capability. It includes active lessons, study, reading/listening for learning and practice. It excludes sleep, unattended immersion, the learner's prior first-language/literacy development and a separate post-learning test session. This is an explicitly assumed second-language acquisition comparison, not a claim that human and model initial representations are identical.

The target is performance on unseen examples of the tested constructions, not memorizing the public test pairs. A 50% two-choice baseline is the operational starting reference. The LSTM's 69.8% is only 39.6% of the possible improvement above chance; it is not 70% of all English proficiency. Likewise, the two roughly 86% models need not have identical mastery of every construction. Match means the human duration targets approximately that aggregate capability by construction, not that a timed human cohort achieved it.

### Primary acquisition evidence and transfer

[Dąbrowska, Becker and Miorelli (2020), Tables 1–2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01839) provide a useful empirical scale: 45 matched classroom learners averaged an estimated 2,220 hours of English instruction and 87% on an 80-item written grammaticality judgment test. They averaged 16 years of education; most had Indo-European first languages. The test covered 28 grammatical subcategories, including agreement, tense and verb subcategorization. Its individual-sentence judgments had a ten-second deadline. Native participants averaged 95%. These are exposure and endpoint observations, not a longitudinal time-to-threshold measurement. Homework and incidental English were not comprehensively counted. Saved original article: `agent-work/sources/language-acquisition/dabrowska2020.html`.

This is not a direct conversion of 87% into BLiMP 87%. BLiMP presents contrasting sentence pairs, has different constructions and vocabulary, and its original individual-human result is 88.6%, versus 96.4% after pooling judgments. Pairwise cues may help, whereas BLiMP's unusual constructions and lexical combinations may hurt. The source sample's education and long learning histories also differ from a typical new learner. These facts support a broad learning-time scale, not a fitted score-to-hours curve.

The [BabyLM second-language paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21254) motivates an explicit-instruction analogue but supplies no human learning timings. [BabySLM](https://github.com/MarvinLvn/BabySLM) restricts syntax to six comparatively simple word-order/agreement phenomena; its examples are not a substitute for BLiMP's binding, raising, island and polarity targets. [DevBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10215) uses scene matching for receptive grammar with children aged 11–12, rather than paired written acceptability. It therefore does not establish a childhood age endpoint for these four models. These alternatives were inspected and inform the limits of the transfer; no child age is converted to waking hours.

### Substantive central estimates

The following budgets are judgments constrained by the source learning scale and the actual construction profiles. They describe overlapping integrated learning activities, partitioned for accounting; they are not independently measured course modules. The foundational work includes enough English vocabulary, decoding and short-sentence comprehension to encounter the benchmark's lexically varied sentences. BLiMP's over-3,000-word lexicon is a scope indicator, not a corpus-word-to-time conversion.

| Active learning component, hours | LSTM target | GPT-BERT 10M target | ELC-BERT 100M / GPT-BERT 100M target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Foundational lexical and short-sentence learning | 450 | 450 | 450 |
| Morphology, agreement, local phrase construction | 250 | 300 | 350 |
| Verb frames, binding, embedded dependencies and licensing | 100 | 450 | 800 |
| Distributed generalization practice and correction | 100 | 400 | 800 |
| **Central total** | **900** | **1,600** | **2,400** |

The LSTM target requires strong recognition of anaphor and irregular-form contrasts, moderate local agreement and partial embedded syntax; it does not require successful island or negative-polarity licensing. Its complex-grammar allowance is consequently an introduction, not mastery. The small GPT-BERT target requires much stronger subject agreement and polarity judgments, with continuing weaknesses in binding, quantifiers and islands. The large-model target requires reliable generalization over most categories and stronger argument/filler-gap distinctions. It merits extensive corrective practice despite remaining well short of perfect performance.

The 2,400-hour upper target is of the same scale as the observed 2,220 classroom hours, allowing some additional active study while narrowing the goal from a general English course to discrimination. It is not obtained by dividing the benchmark percentage by the study percentage. The lower targets substantially reduce advanced dependencies and consolidation, rather than shrinking every aspect proportionally. The small numerical score difference between the two large models does not support a distinct human duration, especially given filtering differences.

Plausible alternative scenarios are 600–1,800 hours for the LSTM, 1,000–3,000 for GPT-BERT small, and 1,600–4,500 for the two large models. Lower scenarios assume efficient targeted instruction and favorable first-language transfer; higher scenarios assume more dispersed study and weaker transfer. They are research scenarios, not confidence intervals. The central estimates concern typical literate learners; the empirical calibration's selected, relatively educated population and the assessment transfer are material limitations. The human evidence is **assumed**, method **estimated**, with no claimed human attempt sample for these new estimates.

## Sources, implementation and compute method

The source papers and original implementation files are freshly retained under `agent-work/sources/`. ELC source tree: `36735c9fac5def145635d80ec82d8b560b5cf865`; GPT-BERT tree: `9fc15074d5e2620ea4ad0871d57454c0f35e3ff0`. The implementation is an architecture/schedule reference; source defaults and example launchers are not runtime logs. The selected models' reported settings take precedence. The portable `calculate.py` reads the retained source configurations and `calculation-inputs.json`, which lists the reported workload inputs, assumptions and source hashes. It requires only the Python 3 standard library. Run `python3 -B calculate.py --sources /path/to/sources/language-acquisition --inputs /path/to/research/language-acquisition/calculation-inputs.json --expected /path/to/agent-work/derived/language-acquisition/calculations.json --output /new/path/calculations.json`. The optional expected file checks every retained calculation field. The output must be new and outside the source directory; the calculator only reads evidence.

For a 12-layer transformer of width H, feed-forward width F and vocabulary V, count multiply-add as two FLOPs. ELC dense projection work per position is `2×12×(4H²+3HF)`; GPT-BERT adds its attention gate, giving `2×12×(5H²+3HF)`. Both have GEGLU feed-forward projections. Ordinary attention products cost `4×12×S×H` per position. ELC computes two relative-position products over 63 buckets, adding `4×12×63×H`; GPT's implementation first expands relative positions and computes two full sequence products, adding another `4×12×S×H`. These distinctions are visible in `elc-model.py` and `gpt-model-extra.py`. The training scripts import the latter GPT module, not its ungated `model.py` alternative.

The recipe also counts dense layer mixing, relative-position projections (a 128-sequence microbatch analogue), normalization/activation/softmax arithmetic, and the classifier only at predicted positions. Masked positions are 15% for ELC. For GPT, approximate the token-weighted declining mask probability as 21%, and combine it with the 1:15 causal-to-masked ratio: predicted fraction `1/16 + 15/16×0.21 = 0.259375`. Its duplicated corpus supports two objectives but does not mean two forward passes for every counted position. Reverse-mode work is approximated as twice the forward work, for three forwards total. Activation checkpoint recomputation is not added: the inspected ELC script disables it and GPT's selected encoder path has no checkpoint call.

LAMB updates, norm/clipping and weight decay use a 20-operation-per-parameter-per-update envelope; GPT's EMA adds three. These small scalar allowances and nonlinear operation approximations are explicit estimates. Shared-model `2N` coefficients are retained for registry consistency but are **not** multiplied by these training tokens; the point uses the component recipe, including the sparse prediction head and attention terms.

Associated monitoring is included separately. The masked models receive one conservative full 67,000-pair BLiMP pass at an assumed 20 processed positions and 20 masks per sentence, including full output projection. This is 53.6M encoder positions, not training tokens. Vocabulary filtering can reduce it. GPT temperature alternatives reuse network logits in `gpt-blimp-score.py`; no independent full forward per temperature is charged. The original GPT-BERT evaluation section and Appendix E select the optimal temperature on BLiMP itself, then transfer that temperature to the held-out supplement. Both GPT-BERT human targets assume unseen pairs, so these rows flag this assessment reuse. The source used to estimate human learning hours does not itself create that flag. Its periodic validation allowance follows one 128×128 batch per worker per 1,000 steps plus final validation, with 128 workers from the example launcher. That worker count is an allowance, not measured deployment evidence. ELC's inspected loop has no separate periodic validation pass. Additional GLUE fine-tuning branches are separate assessments and are unnecessary to reach the zero-shot grammar endpoint, so are excluded. Generic pre-existing dataset creation and the full architecture-development sweep are outside the one-run learning unit.

## lang-lacq-blimp-elcbert100m

[ELC-BERT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02265), Appendix A/Table 4: base model 98M parameters, H=768, F=2048, V=16,384, 15,625 steps, initial global batch 32,768 sequences of 128 positions. The [training implementation](https://github.com/ltgoslo/elc-bert/blob/36735c9fac5def145635d80ec82d8b560b5cf865/train_elc_bert_base.py) quadruples sequence length and quarters sequence batch for the final 10% of steps. Thus **65,536,000,000 training positions**, including repeated corpus passes; mean length 166.4 for attention arithmetic. Treating the 100M-word dataset cap as processed tokens would omit hundreds of passes.

Select **ELC-BERT base**, Table 7, 85.3% BLiMP. Do not substitute the leaderboard's 82.8%, which is a different aggregate, the weighted-output variant's 86.1%, or the later GPT paper's inconsistent summary baseline. In category order AA/AS/B/CR/DNA/E/FG/IF/IE/NL/Q/SVA, its scores are 92.8/81.2/74.0/79.2/96.0/91.7/87.1/93.6/83.9/83.5/70.2/90.8. The human central estimate is **2,400 hours = 8,640,000 seconds**.

Total compute: **36,035,805,328,282,800,128 FLOPs**, including associated evaluation and update arithmetic. This is an analytic point estimate, not precision to the displayed integer's final digit. The [model upload commit](https://huggingface.co/lgcharpe/ELC_BERT_baby_100M/commit/c56dba21e2ed522802f637c881354b7073ec64cf) establishes availability on **2023-08-01**; repository creation alone is not used.

## lang-lacq-blimp-gptbert100m

[GPT-BERT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24159), Section 3 and Appendix A: 119M parameters, H=768, F=2560, V=16,384; 15,625 steps with reported average approximately two million tokens gives **31,250,000,000 training positions**. Sequence lengths progress through 128, 256 and 512. Use a token-weighted mean length 221.952 from the source's 70%/20%/10% step phases weighted by its increasing batch schedule. Final BLiMP is **86.1%**, with optimized temperature and bidirectional scoring. Category scores are 97.7/84.3/74.61/83.7/98.2/86.9/89.3/96.6/77.3/85.2/76.4/95.1. Human central: **2,400 hours = 8,640,000 seconds**.

Total compute: **22,237,947,769,590,644,736 FLOPs**. The token total uses the paper's approximate realized average, not a false exact reconstruction from public defaults. A continuously updated ideal 1M→4M binary batch ramp would average 2,621,440 tokens; that alternative yields about 31% more training compute. The released loader updates batch settings at dataset boundaries and rounds per-device batches, so its nominal linear endpoints do not independently establish the run's realized average. The alternative total is retained in the calculation JSON. This is a substantial workload uncertainty and sets `derived_assumed_inputs`.

The Hugging Face history has a model upload and corrected base configuration on **2024-09-17** ([same-day config correction](https://huggingface.co/ltg/gpt-bert-babylm-base/commit/61699ffc64d75782b19b6f83f7560dcf09966110)); the initial upload temporarily carried a small-model configuration. The corrected file agrees with the paper. The retained `agent-work/sources/language-acquisition/gpt-base-commits.json` distinguishes it from later compatibility edits. This date is model availability, not the later paper date.

## lang-lacq-blimp-gptbert10m

The same source identifies the strict-small model: 30M parameters, H=384, F=1280, V=8,192 and 7,812 steps. The same reported average gives **15,624,000,000 training positions**, not ten million. Total compute: **2,989,285,266,405,301,760 FLOPs**. The same schedule sensitivity and monitoring assumptions apply.

BLiMP **81.2%**, category scores 93.6/78.2/68.8/77.4/97.3/86.1/80.5/91.5/69.8/84.1/68.4/92.2. This is notably stronger local agreement and polarity discrimination than the LSTM, with substantial remaining binding/island/quantifier gaps. Human central: **1,600 hours = 5,760,000 seconds**. The [initial weight upload](https://huggingface.co/ltg/gpt-bert-babylm-small/commit/1998a5426318687f2aa200e613a5e6d2edf1a82e) establishes availability on **2024-09-17**.

## lang-lacq-blimp-lstm

[BLiMP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00582), Table 3 and Section 4, identifies the pretrained Gulordava English LSTM and an 83M-token Wikipedia corpus. [Gulordava et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11138) and its [original supplement](https://github.com/facebookresearch/colorlessgreenRNNs/blob/master/supplementary_material.pdf) give two 650-unit layers, 650-dimensional embeddings, 50K vocabulary and **40 training epochs**. The published English model uses batch 128, dropout 0.2 and learning rate 20; its individual training run, not all 68 exploratory configurations, is the unit. The input embedding is a lookup; it is not charged as a full vocabulary matrix multiplication. Two four-gate recurrent layers cost 13,520,000 matrix FLOPs per position and the dense output 65,000,000, plus 289,000 scalar operations. Training positions: **3,320,000,000**.

The original loop validates once per epoch and evaluates a final test set. Using its approximately 10M-token held-out sets gives 410M validation/test positions; one final BLiMP pass adds 2.68M word positions at the explicit 20-position sentence allowance. SGD/clipping uses five scalar operations per parameter per update, with batch 128 and BPTT length 35 as the source recipe. Total: **817,726,658,352,142,857 FLOPs**. Exact epoch-wise effective corpus/token boundaries are unavailable; 83M and 10M are source-rounded quantities.

BLiMP **69.8%**, category scores 91.7/73.2/73.5/67.0/85.4/67.6/73.9/89.1/46.6/51.7/64.5/80.1. Chance-like island and polarity results are not advanced grammatical mastery. Human central: **900 hours = 3,240,000 seconds**. This target concerns approximately comparable aggregate performance and inspected areas of strength, not reproducing the network's exact errors. The corpus is unfiltered BLiMP, unlike the BabyLM subsets. Original repository evidence establishes public weights, but an exact first availability date for the evaluated revision has not been established; date remains blank.
