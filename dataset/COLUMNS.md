# Column definitions

This file is the hand-maintained specification for the fields of `points.csv`
and `models.csv` and their interpretation. It is the authoritative reference:
if other documentation conflicts, follow this file. Change a column's
definition and the CSVs together, in one commit.

Use the best estimate or classification supported by the available evidence. Imperfect evidence does not by itself make a value unknown. Record the estimate and identify its evidence basis. Optional qualifications belong only in notes; unknown values do not require an explanation. Do not invent missing measurements or sample counts.

## points.csv

| Column | Description | Allowed values and meanings |
|---|---|---|
| `point_id` | Unique identifier for the task–model comparison. | N/A |
| `task` | Short task name. | N/A |
| `task_category` | Primary kind of work covered by the recorded human time and AI compute. Use skill_acquisition for learning comparisons; otherwise classify the whole task, not just its output format, duration, subject or component skills. | `skill_acquisition`: Learning or training to acquire or improve a capability; use when the point represents the learning process rather than subsequent task performance.<br>`writing_media`: Producing or editing text, images, audio or video where composition is the main work; includes translation and summarization of supplied material.<br>`coding`: Implementing, debugging or maintaining software, including testing and deployment needed to deliver it.<br>`perception`: Recognizing or extracting information from sensory input; includes image recognition, OCR and speech transcription.<br>`mathematics_puzzles`: Solving explicit mathematical or rule-based problems; includes arithmetic, proofs and ARC puzzles.<br>`memory_recall`: Retaining and recalling previously encountered information, including supplied material, recall and recognition-memory tests; excludes learning a general skill.<br>`physical_tasks`: Manipulation, locomotion, driving and other physical execution.<br>`games`: Playing board, card or video games, from individual moves to complete games.<br>`research_analysis`: Finding or interpreting information, applying subject knowledge or analyzing evidence to reach answers, findings, judgments or recommendations. Includes scientific questions and substantive research or analysis delivered as reports.<br>`administrative_operational`: Completing business or service workflows; includes scheduling, processing cases, coordinating resources, handling requests and operating a business. |
| `task_description` | A short, common-sense description of what the task is, the way a colleague would describe it over coffee — what a human cares about. One sentence is the target; a few tasks need two. Say what changes the nature of the task (that it is done with a surgical robot on ex vivo tissue; that a document is ~100,000 words; that a proof must be formalized in Lean) and drop everything the human does not care about: harness details (containers, CPUs, memory, timeouts, scorer names, trial counts, "the work unit is one attempt"), feature-by-feature specification lists, and benchmark-internal bookkeeping, which belong in `notes` or the research note. Do not lose facts that distinguish tasks in the same family from each other (grid level, paper count, digit count, target proficiency). Every row sharing a `task_id` carries the identical text, because the description describes the task and not the run — model, scaffold, effort level and other run detail belong in that row's `notes`. Ruled by Damon on 2026-09-16, rewritten to this common-sense standard on 2026-09-17. | N/A |
| `model_id` | Primary model carrying out the task, linked to its record in models.csv. Use one model ID even when other models assist. | N/A |
| `compute_scope` | Whether the primary model is executing a task or being trained. Total compute still includes work by other models: a frozen coding agent fitting a classifier is `inference`, including the classifier fit in its FLOPs. Training scopes include the associated training process, such as rollout generation and evaluation, but not a separate subsequent task-performance run. Initial model and target capability belong in `task_description`. | `inference`: Task execution with fixed primary-model weights, including reasoning, search and repeated forward passes; excludes prior training.<br>`additional_training`: Further training starting from an already trained model; includes fine-tuning or other weight updates and excludes the starting model’s prior training cost.<br>`full_training`: The complete training process from an untrained model to the stated capability, including any subsequent training stages needed to reach it. |
| `compute_flops` | Best estimate of total FLOPs spent by all AI models involved in the work in task_description, including helper calls, at the quality recorded in performance_evidence. Count neural arithmetic at two operations per multiply-add, regardless of execution precision; quantization alone does not remove those operations. The total includes attention over the context, at 4 * attention_layers * attention_width * attention_context per processed position, alongside the weight matrices; a cached prefix position contributes to the context its successors attend over and takes no weights pass. Compute is what was run and not what could have been run: where a source's own per-run billing shows the scaffold recomputed a prefix a cache could have served, the recomputation is charged, as it is on the METR source_total rows (research/metr/cache-accounting.md). A position that takes a weight update rather than a forward pass costs three times each term, 6 * active_parameters and 12 * attention_layers * attention_width * attention_context, which is PaLM's 6N + 12 * L * H * Q * T; the attention term is therefore the same multiple of the weight-matrix term at a training scope as at an inference one, and a row that mixes the two adds each component at its own multiple. The recipe and every assumption behind it are in research/attention-correction.md. The total is not restricted to model_id. Exclude models simulating an external participant or evaluation environment when that role is outside the stated work unit. Estimate missing helper contributions from available evidence; if a helper model is unidentified, the primary model’s coefficient may be used as an explicit assumption, reflected in compute_evidence. It is a parity-compute observation only when performance matches the human baseline. | N/A |
| `compute_flops_low` | Lower end of a range on `compute_flops`, recomputed through that field's own recipe rather than scaled from its central. The range combines exactly two uncertainties and nothing else. The first is the model's `active_parameters_low` to `active_parameters_high` band, which moves the weight-matrix term and, where `attention_basis` is `estimated`, the attention shape that the note's rule derives from the parameter count; a `reported` shape is held fixed at both ends. The second is the attended context, for rows whose `attention_context` came from the cache-implied route: each end takes that row's own cache-read multiple against the 10th and 90th percentile of the new-tokens-per-call constant across the sixteen Terminal-Bench 2.1 submissions, 1,976 and 19,088 against the 2,800 the central uses, which is the rounded earlier median and sits below the 3,041 the consistent inversion returns, bounded above as the central is by half the counted tokens and by the 200,000 cap. Rows whose context rests on the run's own per-call records, on a per-source mean, or on half the counted tokens take no context band, because none of those moves with the constant. On the METR `source_total` rows the processed positions are the counted total itself, because those runs' own per-run billing establishes that the scaffolds recomputed the prefix rather than caching it; that is a determination about what was run, not an uncertainty, and it carries no band. The constant still moves those rows' attended context, through the trajectory length the context is read off, so each end reruns that geometry at that end's n and the bounds are the extremes over the two. What the range does not cover: a token count borrowed from a different work unit, a benchmark aggregate averaged over resolved trials only, the attention-shape rule itself, the choice of cache-implied donor, whether a counted total's cache reads were served from a cache, and every uncertainty on the human side, `human_time` included. The derivation and the constant's percentiles are in research/compute-range/compute-range.md. | Positive number: Lower bound, at or below `compute_flops`.<br>Blank: Neither uncertainty applies, so no bar is asserted. |
| `compute_flops_high` | Upper end of the same range, on the same recipe and the same two uncertainties, at the model's `active_parameters_high` and at the 90th-percentile new-tokens-per-call constant, or on the METR rows at whichever end of the constant returns the larger figure. Blank exactly where `compute_flops_low` is blank. | Positive number: Upper bound, at or above `compute_flops`.<br>Blank: Neither uncertainty applies, so no bar is asserted. |
| `human_skill` | Human population whose time is measured or estimated. For task performance, describe their relevant skill level. For skill acquisition, describe the learners: typical means estimating how long ordinary people would take to learn, not their initial or final proficiency. State starting and target capabilities in task_description. | `typical`: Ordinary people without selection for specialist or elite skill, including people with little or no task-specific training (a former `novice` value, merged 2026-09-16); for skill acquisition, the typical learners whose time to the stated target is being estimated.<br>`expert`: People with substantial relevant training or professional experience.<br>`world_class`: People performing at the highest competitive or professional level. |
| `human_time_scope` | Human activity included in `human_time`. For skill acquisition, the starting skill and target capability belong in `task_description`. | `task_performance`: Time spent carrying out the stated task at the recorded human skill level; excludes prior learning.<br>`skill_acquisition`: Time spent learning or practicing to reach the stated capability from the specified starting skill; excludes a separate subsequent task-performance attempt. |
| `human_time` | Human active time in seconds, for the scope and skill level specified. When the human baseline combines work from several people on a task, sum their active time; do not use parallel elapsed time. This summing applies to task performance only: for skill acquisition, human time is one person's learning time, even when the AI policy plays several roles (one policy controlling five Dota heroes is compared with one player learning the game). For an estimate of reproducing AI work, estimate the time to produce the same quantity of work at comparable quality without AI assistance. Tools needed to build the deliverable remain allowed: a human ML engineer may train a classifier but does not use an AI coding assistant. Record the estimate’s basis in human_time_evidence. **Research-mathematics rows take the standing method of research/math-human-time/math-human-time.md** (Damon, 2026-09-16): size the written mathematics in pages, charge it at the size class's hours per page from that note's table of resolved research problems, and add a formalization term at its measured formal lines per active hour, with the bounds taken from those tables' own dispersion. | N/A |
| `human_time_low` | Lower end of a range on `human_time`, transcribed from the alternative scenarios the row's own research note argues, never scaled from the central by a default factor. What the range carries is the note's own named alternatives: a different anchor, a plausible alternative value of a named factor, or a stated sensitivity interval — MATH Level 5's 5-to-20-minute interval, Crafter's `--fps 10` and `--fps 3` recordings, the Erdős rows' per-anchor bands and multipliers, the Blender cabin's half-to-double scenarios, LUMEN's variants table. Where the note argues the central is itself a floor or a ceiling, that bound sits at `human_time`; the central need not be the midpoint, because it sits where the note put it. Only rows whose `human_time_evidence` is `llm_estimate_judgment` or `llm_estimate_from_data` are eligible: a `task_timings`, `defined_duration` or `source_estimate` duration is someone else's measurement or estimate, and this dataset does not invent uncertainty on it. What the range does not cover: the choice of anchor itself, the task-to-donor transfer, the matched-quality target, and every uncertainty on the compute side. The per-row mapping from bound to note sentence is research/human-range/human-range.csv and the rule is research/human-range/human-range.md. | Positive number: Lower bound, at or below `human_time`.<br>Blank: The row is ineligible, or its note argues no scenarios, so no bar is asserted. |
| `human_time_high` | Upper end of the same range, from the same note's other scenario, on the same eligibility. Blank exactly where `human_time_low` is blank. | Positive number: Upper bound, at or above `human_time`.<br>Blank: The row is ineligible, or its note argues no scenarios, so no bar is asserted. |
| `performance_vs_human` | Best supported comparison with the human baseline; formal statistical equivalence is not required. Relevant results from a broader task collection may support an estimated comparison; identify the transfer in performance_evidence. Selecting successful human timings does not imply that humans always succeed. When human time explicitly estimates reproducing the AI output at comparable quality, use match by construction. Estimated time alone does not imply match. A row whose AI performance is not comparable to the human's at all is not labelled: it is withheld from the dataset (see the note below this table). | `below`: AI performs noticeably worse than the human baseline, but basically does the job: it completed the job at lower quality, or scored at least about half the human's score on the benchmark's own metric after subtracting the chance floor.<br>`match`: Reported performance is broadly comparable, or the human-time estimate explicitly targets work of comparable quality to the AI output.<br>`above`: AI performs noticeably or decisively better than the human baseline. It covers both a modest edge and the case where the human basically does not do the job by comparison: the AI beat champions or experts by a step no human in the sample matched, or the human scores less than about half the AI on the benchmark's own metric after subtracting the chance floor. |
| `comparison_issues` | Known differences affecting the AI–human comparison, detailed in `notes`. Multiple values are separated by `; `. Missing documentation or an estimated human baseline alone is not a known difference; flag a concrete difference in the compared tasks or conditions. Blank means the comparison has not been assessed; it does not mean no issues exist. | `none_identified`: Available task and assessment evidence has been reviewed and no material difference identified; use alone. Does not certify identical conditions in every detail.<br>`different_task`: Different task content or quantities of work, including a substitute deliverable used because the original task lacks required inputs.<br>`different_inputs_or_tools`: Different supplied information or permitted tools.<br>`different_assessment`: Different scoring criteria, evaluation sets or assessment protocols.<br>`different_human_baseline`: Human timing and performance evidence concern different skill groups or populations.<br>`different_attempt_selection`: AI compute and human time select different outcome subsets, such as all versus successful attempts. |
| `compute_evidence` | Evidence supporting the FLOP value. Applies to the source’s underlying calculation as well as our own; shared parameter assumptions are in models.csv; point-specific assumptions and accounting limitations belong in notes. Use transferred_workload when the main workload is borrowed from a different work unit, even if its original measurement or arithmetic is sound. Estimating this task’s workload from its own quantities or constraints is derived_assumed_inputs. | `transferred_workload`: Main workload borrowed from another task or a broader collection average, rather than established for this point’s stated work. An average for a point explicitly describing that same source-defined collection is not a transfer.<br>`measured_operations`: Operations measured for the stated work.<br>`derived_supported_inputs`: Calculated using reported or measured inputs to a documented method, without a substantial assumed input.<br>`derived_assumed_inputs`: Calculated using a substantial assumed input, such as model size, token count or hardware utilization. |
| `human_time_evidence` | What the duration rests on, separately from the performance label. Read it as a ladder of how much measurement stands behind the number and who set it: measured on this task; fixed by the work unit; estimated by the source; estimated by the dataset builder from measured timings elsewhere; estimated by the dataset builder by judgment. A matched-quality target does not make its estimated duration measured. Revised 2026-09-13 by Damon's ruling (formerly task_timings / transferred_timings / defined_duration / assumed / unknown). | `task_timings`: Recorded timings of this task by people of the stated skill level, including aggregates and unit conversions of them.<br>`defined_duration`: The work unit itself fixes the duration, such as one minute of real-time play or a four-hour contest window; no human completion-time sample is implied. A source saying how long a task should take is a source_estimate, not a defined duration.<br>`source_estimate`: The source estimated the duration and published it: task authors' expected completion times, or estimates by experts the source recruited. No timing observations, or none the source discloses.<br>`llm_estimate_from_data`: The dataset builder, an LLM agent, estimated the duration by mapping recorded timings of another task or population onto this one. The sample columns describe the donor sample. A study cited only as context for a judgment does not qualify.<br>`llm_estimate_judgment`: The dataset builder estimated the duration by judgment, with no timing observations feeding the number; includes its estimates of reproducing AI work at comparable quality and work-rate assumptions with no measured rate behind them. |
| `performance_evidence` | Performance criterion and available AI and human results. For a matched-quality time estimate, state the human target as an assumed baseline, not an observed human result. Success counts include attempted totals. No sources or citations; use source_record. Qualifications belong in notes. | N/A |
| `human_time_statistic` | Statistic defining `human_time`. Arithmetic mean is preferred; other statistics are retained without conversion. | `mean`: Arithmetic average of the included human times.<br>`median`: Middle ordered human time, averaging the middle pair for an even count.<br>`geometric_mean`: Exponential of the mean log of positive human times.<br>`median_of_task_medians`: Median across tasks of their median human times.<br>`point_estimate`: A single estimate without a sample-statistic interpretation. |
| `human_time_subset` | Attempts included in the time statistic. This is a selection rule, not the human success rate. Selecting a skill group alone is not a successful-attempt filter. For transferred timings, apply this selection rule to the contributing donor attempts, even when the target duration is estimated. | `all`: All recorded attempts, including failures and timeouts.<br>`successful`: Only attempts meeting the stated success criterion.<br>`not_applicable`: No selection among recorded attempts applies, for example an analytic duration estimate. |
| `human_attempts` | Number of recorded human attempts in the subset identified by human_time_subset. For successful, count successful attempts; for all, count all attempts. Count known attempts even if their timings do not reproduce a source summary; record that discrepancy separately in notes. For transferred timings, count known contributing donor attempts, including timing-model calibration records; identify the donor in notes. This is not an effective sample size for the target-task estimate. Do not count a performance-only sample. | Positive integer: Recorded attempt count.<br>`not_applicable`: Human duration is an estimate without a contributing sample of recorded human attempts.<br>Blank: A contributing sample exists, but its count is unknown. |
| `human_time_source` | Citation for the recorded human timings or the research-note evidence and calculation behind the human-time estimate. | N/A |
| `compute_method` | Method used to obtain FLOPs. This field contains one categorical value, never a formula. Evidence for inputs is classified separately in compute_evidence. | `reported`: FLOP value taken directly from a source.<br>`params_tokens`: Sum each model’s token count * its FLOPs-per-token coefficient. For one model, this is tokens * flops_per_token from models.csv. For multiple models, retain the breakdown in compute_source; do not automatically apply the primary model’s coefficient to all tokens. When cache counts are available, count fresh input, cache creation and output; exclude cache reads from this parameter-multiplication term. Attention over the context is added on top of that term rather than omitted, per research/attention-correction.md; where a native total includes cache reads, the processed-position count is rebuilt before the coefficient is applied. If cache counts are unavailable, use a documented processing estimate supported by the implementation evidence; otherwise state the source-total or full-prefix assumption. Excluding cache reads requires evidence that they were served from a cache: where a per-run billing figure shows the prefix was recomputed, the counted total is the processed total. Include material padding or repeated forwards implied by the same implementation.<br>`operation_count`: Calculate operations from architecture and workload inputs; includes training estimates such as 6 * parameters * training_tokens plus associated inference compute. Record the arithmetic and assumptions in the linked research note.<br>`hardware_time`: Hardware FLOPs per second * runtime in seconds * utilization; record inputs in the linked research note. |
| `compute_statistic` | Statistic defining `compute_flops`. Arithmetic mean is preferred; other statistics are retained without conversion. | `mean`: Arithmetic average of the included attempt compute values.<br>`median`: Middle ordered compute value, averaging the middle pair for an even count.<br>`total`: Total compute for the whole work unit described; not necessarily a single attempt.<br>`total_per_completed_sample`: Aggregate compute divided by completed samples; the numerator may also include incomplete samples. This is not necessarily mean compute per successful attempt.<br>`point_estimate`: A single estimate without a sample-statistic interpretation. |
| `compute_subset` | Attempts included in the compute statistic. This is a selection rule, not the AI success rate. | `all`: All recorded attempts, including failures and timeouts.<br>`successful`: Only attempts meeting the stated success criterion.<br>`positive_tokens`: Only attempts with positive token counts, regardless of outcome.<br>`not_applicable`: No selection among recorded attempts applies, for example an operation-count recipe. |
| `ai_attempts` | Count of AI task runs represented by an attempt-level compute statistic, after compute_subset selection. A continuous goal run counts as one attempt, including intermediate retries and sessions. Estimating missing compute inputs does not erase a known task-run count. Does not count turns, tokens or questions used to normalize a benchmark aggregate. | Positive integer: Applicable recorded task-run count.<br>`not_applicable`: Compute is an analytic estimate or a benchmark aggregate normalized per question, rather than a statistic over defined task runs.<br>Blank: An applicable task-run count exists but is unknown. |
| `compute_source` | Citation for the compute inputs or directly reported FLOP value, including the research-note calculation where derived. | N/A |
| `tokens` | Text-token workload used in the compute estimate, with the counted token types specified by tokens_accounting. For inference, total the included model-token counts. For training, record training tokens only, including repeated passes; record separate rollout and evaluation workloads in the linked research note and include their FLOPs in compute_flops. For multiple models, compute_source records the per-model counts and coefficients. Applies to params_tokens and to token-dependent operation_count estimates. May be measured or assumed, as recorded in compute_evidence; a derivation belongs in the linked research note, with material assumptions summarized in notes. Do not combine audio positions with text tokens into one undifferentiated count. Image patches, audio frames and continuous action positions stay in the research-note calculation rather than being added to text tokens. Use not_applicable when no text-token count enters the method; blank means a needed count is unknown. | Number: Token count, including fractional summary statistics.<br>`not_applicable`: Compute method does not use a token count. |
| `tokens_accounting` | What is counted in tokens. This field defines the counted quantity, not whether it was measured or assumed. | `training`: Tokens processed during weight-update training, including repeated epochs; excludes separate inference, rollout and evaluation tokens, whose compute is accounted for in the research-note calculation.<br>`source_total`: Native source total whose decomposition is unspecified.<br>`input_output`: Input tokens plus output tokens.<br>`input_cache_creation_output`: Input tokens plus cache-creation tokens plus output tokens; cache reads excluded.<br>`encoder_processed`: Text-token positions processed by an encoder-only model, including tokenizer special positions and repeated passes; excludes predicted labels and non-text positions.<br>`decoder_processed`: Text-token positions processed by a decoder-only or encoder-decoder model; excludes audio/encoder positions. Includes prompt, special, padding or repeated positions when those forwards enter the compute estimate.<br>`not_applicable`: Compute method does not use a token count. |
| `source_dataset` | Name of the source collection or study. | N/A |
| `source_record` | Identifiers or locators for the source records, task selection and model configuration used for the point. Includes citations supporting performance results, prefixed with performance: where needed to identify their purpose. | N/A |
| `notes` | Optional concrete information needed to interpret this point that is not already captured in another field. Leave blank when there is nothing to add. No repeated values, editing history, generic uncertainty statements or explanations required merely because a field is unknown. | N/A |
| `ai_cost_usd` | Dollar cost of the AI work, same unit and statistic as `compute_flops`. Includes cached reads and helpers. Never estimated. | Positive number, or blank |
| `ai_cost_basis` | Source of the figure. | `reported`: the operator or paper states the dollar amount spent.<br>`list_price`: the source states token counts and they are priced at the provider's published list rates on `ai_cost_date`.<br>`not_available`: the source states neither a dollar amount nor token counts, or the payment cannot be isolated to this workload. |
| `ai_cost_date` | Date whose prices apply: the run date, or the price-sheet date where the run date is unknown. | `YYYY-MM-DD`, or blank when the basis is `not_available` |
| `human_cost_usd` | Dollar figure the source reports for the human doing the same unit and statistic as `human_time`. Never derived from time and a wage. | Positive number, or blank |

| `attention_context` | Mean number of context positions a processed position attended over, used for the attention part of compute_flops. Set from the run's own per-call or per-turn records where the source publishes them, otherwise from a per-source mean derived from turn or call counts, otherwise as a cache-implied mean prefix, cache reads over counted tokens times 2,800 new tokens per call, taken from the run's own cached-against-uncached split or transferred from a same-model run in a comparable agentic harness that publishes one, otherwise as half the counted tokens, which is the length-weighted mean prefix of an append-only dialog processed once. Where a counted total is inclusive of cache reads that the source's own billing shows were not served from a cache, every counted position took a weights pass and the mean attended context is read off the trajectory geometry instead, T * ((P - T) / 3 + T / 2) / P for a final trajectory of length T; that is the METR source_total rows, and the derivation is in research/attention-correction.md#metr-cache-reads. A transferred estimate is preferred to falling straight to the cap. Where the value would exceed 200,000 it is held there and the row's correction is an upper bound; the cap binds the result of every tier, a measured turn-count figure included, because its justification is that an agent harness compacts rather than grows past a model's window. The one exception is a single published reasoning dialog with no compacting harness behind it. The basis for each value is tabulated in research/attention-correction.md#mean-attended-context. On a training row it is the mean attended context of a causal pass over the training sequence, (S + 1) / 2 for a packed length-S sequence, length-weighted where the sequence length changed during the run, and weighted by each component's weight-matrix FLOPs where the training and monitoring sequences differ. It covers only positions the row's own model processes: a helper model's attention enters compute_flops at the helper's own shape and context and not this field. The training sequence lengths and their sources are tabulated in research/attention-correction.md#training-rows. | Positive number: Assumed mean attended context in tokens.<br>Blank: No attention term is added to this row. |
| `attention_ratio` | Attention FLOPs as a multiple of the weight-matrix FLOPs they accompany, equal to 2 * attention_layers * attention_width * attention_context / active_parameters. Dividing compute_flops by 1 + attention_ratio recovers the parameter-only value for a params_tokens row whose processed-position count did not change. It is a property of the model shape and the assumed context, not of the row's token count. | Positive number: Estimated ratio.<br>Blank: No attention term is added to this row. |
| `task_id` | Identifier for the task itself, shared by every row that covers the same unit of work. Rows differing only in the model, its scaffold, its effort or reasoning level, or another configuration of the same run are the same task and take one `task_id`; a different unit of work — a different quantity, a different subset of a benchmark, or a different deliverable — takes its own, even where the `task` name is otherwise the same. One exception, ruled by Damon on 2026-09-16: where a row's `human_time` is defined by the level the AI itself reached — a skill-acquisition row timing a human learning to that run's proficiency, or a row whose human time estimates reproducing that configuration's own output at comparable quality — the row is its own task and is never merged with another configuration's row, because configurations can only compete for one frontier tag against one human duration. It is asserted, not computed: `task` carries the configuration in its name on some rows and not on others, so the identifier says which rows belong together rather than leaving it to be read out of the prose. Slugs come from the task name; a new row on a task already in the file reuses that task's existing value. Grouping by this field, or filtering to `frontier`, gives the one-row-per-task view. | N/A |
| `frontier` | Whether this row is the task's frontier: the cheapest row that reaches the human baseline. Derived from the other columns by `research/frontier/frontier.py`, never set by hand. Match is the target and not a ceiling — the cheapest `match` row of a task wins over both a cheaper `below` row and any `above` row, and a task with no `match` row takes its cheapest `above` row. A tie on `compute_flops` within the winning label goes to the lower `point_id`. Ruled by Damon on 2026-09-16; the derivation is in `research/frontier/frontier.md`. | `yes`: This row is its task's frontier.<br>`no`: Another row of this task is the frontier, or the task has no frontier because none of its rows reaches `match` or `above`.<br>Blank: The task is not eligible, because its rows do not agree on `human_time` and the comparable unit of work is therefore unsettled. |

**A row's FLOPs must be an estimate for that row's task.** A collection average
is an estimate for the collection, not for any task inside it. Where the only
compute evidence is a per-task average over a whole source collection, the
collection gets one row — positioned at its mean human time, with the average as
its compute — and the individual tasks get no rows at all. Copying the average
onto each task would assert a per-task measurement that does not exist, and would
weight the collection by however many tasks it happens to contain. Ruled by Damon
on 2026-09-14 and applied to GDPval, whose 440 task rows became
`work-gdpval-collection-gpt5` and `work-gdpval-collection-opus5`.

Cost columns: observed only, never estimated. The Codex-collected rows were backfilled from `research/codex-cost-backfill.csv`.


**`performance_vs_human` and what is left out.** `below` means the AI basically
does the job, somewhat worse than the human; `above` means the AI is better,
whether by a modest edge or by a step where the human basically does not do the
job by comparison. There is deliberately no `far_below`. A row substantially below the human
baseline is not given a label at all: it is withheld from `points.csv`, because
a catastrophic failure says little about how hard a task is whereas a near miss
does. Withheld rows and the reasons for withholding them are kept outside the
product, in `agent-work/removed/`. The numerical guide for the `below` floor is
half the human's score on the benchmark's own metric after subtracting the
chance floor, but the call is a judgment about whether the job was done, not a
rigid rule. The dispositions and the rule as applied are in
`research/performance-relabel.md`, with the machine-readable overrides in
`research/performance-relabel.csv`; both predate 2026-09-15, when the separate
`far_above` label was merged into `above` because 15 rows against 126 is not a
distinction the data supports.

**One row per task.** A task usually appears several times over, once per
model and sometimes once per configuration of a model, so a count over
`points.csv` counts models and not tasks. `task_id` says which rows are the same
task and `frontier` marks the one row per task that reaches the human baseline
most cheaply, which is the row to keep when the question is about tasks. The
frontier is the cheapest `match` row, or the cheapest `above` row where the task
has no `match` row; a task every one of whose rows sits `below` the human has no
frontier row, because nothing on it has reached the baseline yet. Tasks whose
rows disagree on `human_time` are left untagged: the rows are pairing the same
work against different human durations, and which duration the task should carry
is unsettled. No task is in that position: the twenty-two that were are all
settled, and the record of each is in `research/frontier/frontier.md`. Ruled by
Damon on 2026-09-16.
